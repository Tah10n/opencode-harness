# Revision 2: the selector became less reliable

**The revised selector delivered 1 acceptable patch out of 6, versus 4/6 in the historical selection series. It again accepted the incomplete dual-config patch, returned no correct NEITHER, and introduced completion failures. No additional usable delivery was obtained.** The six known V3 pairs remain development data; neither this result nor the earlier 4/6 establishes general harness lift. This revision is not ready for the next quality evaluation or promotion.

Exactly six new attempts used **OpenCode 1.18.26, openai/gpt-5.6-luna, high, 300 seconds overall**, with tools/research limited to 240 seconds. Selector revision `088393068e0b26894d5a00fd2362938b0addfb7d`, prompt, balanced X/Y mapping, task order and exact final patches were frozen before the first request. There were no new author solutions, H1 changes, patch repairs, retries, third candidates, other models, or grader disclosures. The [historical series](README.md) and its patches are unchanged. The [protocol and local validation](REVISION-2.md) describe the single revision; [structured results](results-revision-2.json) record the hashes, resource accounting, outcomes and operator interventions.

These columns measure different things. The mechanism emitted **2/6 valid completed decisions**, only **1/6** selecting acceptable bytes; the other selected an incomplete patch. **3/6 native answers** finished normally within the deadline, but protocol-error-api failed the new record-consistency check. Correct rejection of the both-incomplete pair was **0/1**. A record saying “missing” about one candidate is not itself successful rejection of the entire pair. There were no completed INSUFFICIENT decisions. A rejection would not count as a delivered user task.

The existing inventory still permits at most **five acceptable deliveries and one justified rejection**. This is an external bound, never a model hint. Main historical generation acceptance remains **P 4/6 and H 4/6**. Under the unchanged **selector-export sensitivity**, dropping the disputed omitted-argument export requirement makes catalog X/H acceptable too and gives historical H 5/6 versus P 4/6. Revised delivery remains **1/6**, the bound remains five, and catalog becomes a both-acceptable tie with no established advantage. The dual-config contract is not reinterpreted.

**Acceptance records did not solve semantic completeness.** On dual-config, Luna again identified Y/P's missing negative tests through the real consumers, yet called X/H complete. Direct inspection of the unchanged supplied tests confirms that X tests CJS rendering with `pretty + indent: 0`, but ESM rendering only with indent 4; its old ESM compact-string assertion is also gone. Both are explicit delivery requirements. The selector's green external probe covers the runtime case through both consumers, but does not add the missing regression to the delivered patch. Its “confirmed” record therefore passed a syntactic consistency check while remaining semantically false.

**The record contract also lost an otherwise usable selection.** In protocol-error-api, the native answer finished and explicitly preferred X/P, but added the expressly excluded full web/database/platform matrix as an unverified RESULT for both candidates. The reader treats every RESULT as mandatory, so its consistency rule rejected the choice. The same out-of-scope row appears in extraction, independently of that attempt's lifecycle failure. This is a model decomposition error amplified by the record contract, not a new missing product requirement. These outputs are retained; their records are not reclassified or repaired after seeing results.

**The reserved final stage failed to make completion reliable.** On legacy, identical scratch diagnostics again demonstrated the retained-launcher failure on X/P and a successful migration on Y/H. Research stopped after its outstanding request settled at 269.6 seconds; the same native session received the preplanned tool-free final prompt with about 30.4 seconds left. No final text arrived before 300 seconds. Its last cancelled provider request has unknown usage and server completion under the existing overall-deadline accounting rules.

On reconnect and extraction, the research timer waited for the outstanding provider response to end, then killed the native process immediately. Text containing DECISION had reached the event stream, but native `step_finish: stop` and normal process completion had not. The phase logic detected the emitted decision and correctly refused to ask for a repair, yet the interrupted phase could not pass its terminal requirement. Both remain unfinished; the report does not extract successful choices from unterminated native output. This observed lifecycle defect was not covered by the scripted crossing-boundary case, whose research response deliberately had no DECISION.

The scheduler labelled those two research timeouts `unattributed_timeout` and paused after slots 4 and 5. Before admitting each remaining slot, the operator verified finished provider streams, no refusal, unchanged inputs, verified local termination, closed forwarding and removed containers. **Two documented operator resumptions admitted only unstarted slots (5–6, then 6)**, as required for ordinary choice errors. No first-four or first-five result was retried or rewritten. Prompt, selection phases, native plugin, candidate bytes and frozen runner source were unchanged; local scheduler copies only skipped completed slots and used separate pause records. This was not an unattended six-run scheduler success, and the intervention is part of the negative result.

The experiment ends here. The revision adds no acceptable delivery, still accepts an incomplete candidate and loses timely completion. No additional formulation, longer deadline, third candidate, new evaluation, merge, release or default change follows from this result.
