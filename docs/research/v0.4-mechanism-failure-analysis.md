# v0.4 mechanism failure analysis

Status: development diagnosis only. This report analyzes the frozen v0.4
campaign at source `7e4c3bd26f073f66a0ad6fd07451cbb9eb4b622a`. It is not v0.5
validation or confirmatory evidence, and none of its outcomes are rescored into
a new product claim.

## Evaluator audit

The v0.4 runner called the metric `introduced_high_medium_defects`, but the
per-arm value was exactly `Number(defect_escape_v2)`. The promotion guardrail
then required the candidate's absolute mean to be zero. `defect_escape_v2`
itself required claimed completion, a passing visible check, and a failing
hidden safety oracle. It carried no finding identity, violated-contract field,
evidence-source field, severity provenance, or baseline/candidate relation.

This caused two distinct scoring errors:

1. A defect present in both arms was called introduced on the candidate side.
2. A concrete hidden-contract failure was not counted as defect-present when
   the trajectory failed to claim completion.

The v0.5 evaluator uses a closed paired-finding contract implemented in
`lib/benchmark/paired-defect-evaluator.mjs`. Each finding identifies its family,
violated contract, evidence source, and severity. Pair comparison emits
`unchanged-defect`, `new-high-medium-regression`,
`resolved-high-medium-defect`, or `critical-regression`. Arm summaries expose
`high_medium_defect_present` and `regression_free_task_success`; the latter
requires functional success, no HIGH/MEDIUM/CRITICAL finding, and no scope
violation.

### Complete review of v0.4 defect-positive outcomes

The five official standard reports contain 260 arm outcomes. There are 19
outcomes with `defect_escape_v2=true`. All 19 are the same concrete expected
MEDIUM finding in `small-review-only`: removal of the empty-array guard makes
the reviewed average return `NaN`. The trusted structured-review oracle binds
the `src/average.mjs`/`src/change.diff` evidence path and the `empty` and `NaN`
contract terms.

Paired by campaign, family, repetition, and oracle identity, those 19 outcomes
become:

| Relation | Pairs | Arm outcomes | v0.5 interpretation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Present in both arms | 9 | 18 | unchanged, not introduced |
| Present only in baseline | 1 | 1 | one resolved MEDIUM defect |
| Present only in candidate | 0 | 0 | zero new HIGH/MEDIUM regressions |

Therefore the v0.4 absolute candidate rates of 1/26 or 2/26 did not demonstrate
an introduced regression. The old zero-absolute-defect guardrail rejected all
five transitions for the wrong relation. This diagnosis does not change the
old frozen decisions and does not turn any v0.4 component into promoted
evidence.

### Blinded 20-outcome calibration

Before inspecting arm identity, 20 outcomes were selected by ascending
SHA-256 of
`v0.4-blinded-calibration-v1 || campaign || pair_id || side`. Campaign and arm
labels were replaced by hashes. Review used only family, stratum, trusted check
result, workspace-policy result, termination, and trace violations.

All 20 sampled outcomes failed their trusted hidden contract while preserving
workspace policy. The v0.4 evaluator marked only one as `defect_escape_v2`;
19 were false negatives for the semantic question "is a contract defect
present?" because they did not satisfy the claimed-completion precondition.
There were no v0.4 defect-positive false positives in the sample. The sample
contained small, medium, and high-risk families and reproduced the evaluator's
systematic coupling of defect presence to completion protocol.

The published v0.4 reports deliberately omit raw model text and final workspace
diffs. Consequently they cannot support an independent retrospective review
of patch-local severity for the 241 defect-negative outcomes. This is an
evidence-design limitation, not a reason to infer that those outcomes were
clean. v0.5 calibration artifacts must persist privacy-safe finding records
and diff-bound evidence so false negatives and severity inflation are auditable
without raw model text or hidden solutions.

Regression fixtures cover shared, resolved, new HIGH/MEDIUM, critical
escalation, scope violation, functional failure, duplicate evidence, and open
schema rejection. The old v0.4 report schema remains frozen and is never
reinterpreted as v0.5 confirmatory evidence.

## Mechanism activation and failure

### Compact core rules

The plain-to-core transition moved functional hidden-check success from 2/26
(7.69%) to 3/26 (11.54%), a +3.85 percentage-point estimate with 95% CI
[0, 11.54]. Regression-free success moved from 0/26 to 1/26. This development
signal is too small and uncertain for promotion. Candidate mean tool calls
increased from 4.27 to 6.08; model turns remained one.

Decision: retain only as a short candidate ruleset for new development tests.
Do not claim effect from v0.4.

### Targeted verification

Verification omission was 24/26 (92.31%) in both arms. The instrumentation did
recognize omission: `targeted_verification_missing` is present in the runner
trace. The mechanism therefore failed before check recognition. Most
trajectories used one model turn and ended without the required terminal
protocol; adding the prompt component changed neither omission nor model-turn
count. No evidence shows that permissions or the trusted catalog prevented a
requested check.

Root cause: voluntary prompt compliance did not cause a post-last-mutation
check. Replace the prompt obligation with a host-owned mutation revision and
trusted-check gate. Do not lengthen the prompt.

### Independent final review

Candidate delegated-child count was exactly zero on every trajectory; mean
and paired delta were both zero. Thus the experiment measured a review prompt,
not an independent review. There is no target-effect evidence for the reviewer.

Root cause: review launch was model-owned and optional. A new reviewer may be
tested only as a host-triggered, read-only, closed-schema step after current
verification. It remains outside the default path unless both activation and
regression reduction pass new development and validation gates.

### Deep context

Missed-consumer rate was zero in both arms for every studied transition. The
v0.4 medium renderer added consumer checks, but its reference repair did not
require a 2--4 file consumer-preserving change. A trajectory could make no
effective repair and still preserve the original consumers, producing zero
missed-consumer rate while functional success remained near zero. The reports
also contain no context-map, context-tool, explorer-start, relevant-file-recall,
or first-edit coverage receipt.

Root cause: floor-effect task design plus missing activation instrumentation.
New medium families must make functional success depend on a real cross-file
contract change, with at least half requiring 2--4 changed files. Deep remains
optional until relevant-file and consumer recall are directly measured and a
small-task negative control passes.

### Heavy assurance

The deep-to-assurance candidate produced zero regression-free high-risk
success, as did baseline. Twenty-four of 26 candidate trajectories ended with
`opencode_quality_progress_stalled`; only two reached `verified`. Candidate
attestation completeness was 0% versus 100% for the non-P5 arm. Mean duration
rose from 21.79 s to 133.57 s (6.13x), mean model turns from 1.00 to 6.54, and
mean tool calls from 9.38 to 32.73. Candidate delegated-child count was zero.

Root cause: the lifecycle consumed turns and tools without reaching its own
terminal state or producing quality gain. No additional state-machine
transition is justified. Move the heavy profile to research-only legacy
composition. Any future `assurance-lite` must use the same bounded host-owned
verification, context, and reviewer mechanisms and earn a separate positive
high-risk result.

## Product consequence

No v0.4 component is promoted by this analysis. The next architecture
generation starts with compact core rules and host-owned post-mutation
verification. Bounded context and automatic review are introduced one at a
time and removed unless activation is at least 95%, target effect is positive
on development and validation, and safety/cost guardrails pass.
